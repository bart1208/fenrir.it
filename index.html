About

It started with an Intel 386 and never really stopped. Twenty years later the through-line is still the same one: I like understanding a system all the way down, and I like the part where you get your hands dirty — hardware, electronics, a soldering iron, code that runs on a machine you can hear.

The professional record is deliberately broad — public administration, a global agency, enterprise consulting, video games, and six years in regulated sports betting. I used to think of that as a lack of focus. I have come round to the opposite view: generality is a skill, and it is the thing that makes a new direction credible instead of opportunistic. Somebody who has only ever shipped one kind of software claiming they now do AI is guessing. I have shipped six kinds.

What I care about now is narrower. AI agents are being handed real access to real systems far faster than anyone is working out how they should be identified, authorised and audited. That gap is interesting, it is unglamorous, and it is exactly the kind of problem twenty years of operations makes you good at.

Outside the screen: two sons who get most of the good ideas, a workshop, and a standing preference for things built over things assembled.
